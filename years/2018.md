The first ImageCLEF VQA-Med challenge and VQA-RAD approached medical visual question answering (Med-VQA) from different directions. ImageCLEF built questions and answers from biomedical figures and captions, then asked people to review them. The teams could compare results using common data splits and scoring rules. The challenge compared answers with the reference in several ways, including word overlap and similarity between medical concepts. VQA-RAD started with radiology teaching images and asked people with clinical training to write or rephrase questions. It includes both questions with a limited set of answers and more open questions. The rephrased questions let researchers check whether a model can give the same answer when asked in different words. How the questions were written matters. A question derived from a caption can reflect what the article chose to describe; a question about a teaching image is limited to what that view shows. Neither task follows a clinician through a full examination or gives the model a complete patient history. And matching the words in a reference answer does not guarantee that the model got the finding right. Together, the two studies give readers a starting point for comparing how Med-VQA datasets are built and how answers are scored.
